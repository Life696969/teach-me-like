# Scene 05: Twenty heads in a row

- **Episode:** *The Mentalist*, S2E14 "Blood In, Blood Out" (CBS, first aired 11 Feb 2010)
- **Used in:** Lesson 6
- **Confidence:** the coin flips and the bet are confirmed by the fan wiki; the reveal (two quarters, one double-headed) is confirmed by an episode recap. Both are consistent.
- **Tell it like this:** in your own words, never as dialogue. Spoiler level: none (it's a side story).

## What happens

In Lisbon's office, Jane flips a quarter twenty times, and it lands heads every single time. Rigsby bets twenty dollars against it, loses, and spends the rest of the episode demanding to know how it was done. Jane claims supernatural powers. Later, at a diner, he finally shows Rigsby the boring truth: he had two quarters, one with heads on both sides, and he swapped them.

**Stop point (for lessons):** stop after the twentieth heads. Ask: *"Twenty heads in a row. List every possible explanation, from most boring to most magical."*

## What Jane noticed

People reach for an impressive explanation before a boring one, especially when someone *tells* them it's special.

## What he did

1. A **switch**: a small, hidden action (swapping coins).
2. **Misdirection by story**: claiming powers points attention at the wrong question ("how can he do that?") instead of the right one ("is that a normal coin?").

## The technique

- **Misdirection:** control where attention goes, so the real method happens somewhere nobody is looking. Here the "somewhere" is an assumption: that the coin is fair.
- **Defence: suspect the simple explanation.** When something looks impossible, check the setup before you believe the miracle.

## Science check

- **The maths:** a fair coin landing heads 20 times in a row has odds of 1 in 2^20, about **1 in a million** (1 in 1,048,576). Suspect the coin, not physics.
- Magicians' misdirection is a real, studied toolkit for steering attention (Kuhn et al. 2008), and attention is narrow enough that about half of viewers in a famous study missed a person in a gorilla suit walking through a scene (Simons & Chabris 1999).
- **The honest part:** many TV "reads" in this genre are tricks with a hidden method. The show itself admits it here, which makes this a perfect scene to learn from.

## Try it

Learn one simple coin vanish or a "guess which cup" with a hidden method, perform it for a friend, and narrate the misdirection afterwards: where did their attention go at each second?
