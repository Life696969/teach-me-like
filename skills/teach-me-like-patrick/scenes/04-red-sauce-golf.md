# Scene 04: The golf tip and the birthday guesses

- **Episode:** *The Mentalist*, S1E20 "Red Sauce" (CBS, first aired 28 Apr 2009)
- **Used in:** Lesson 5 (the golf course); the birthday guesses also work as a warm-up in Lesson 2
- **Confidence:** both moments are confirmed by at least two sources.
- **Tell it like this:** in your own words, never as dialogue. Spoiler level: low.

## Moment A: the birthday guesses

At Lisbon's office birthday party, Jane correctly calls what each teammate has bought her before she opens it: a fitness item from one, a spa voucher from another (he wins a small bet on that one), and a bottle of bubbly from the third. Lisbon assumes he's forgotten her present. He tells her it's on its way.

**What it teaches:** base rates plus personal knowledge look like mind reading. People buy a boss a narrow range of "safe" gifts, and each colleague's personality narrows it further.

## Moment B: the golf course

A murdered arcade worker turns out to have been a mob informant living under witness protection. Jane walks up, alone, to the retired mob boss on a golf course, and is nearly shot. He stays calm and gives the man reasons to listen, one of them being that he can fix the man's swing. The tip works. The boss relaxes, talks about the informant, reveals that he had no idea about the killing, and hands Jane his private number.

**Stop point (for lessons):** stop when Jane is facing an angry, armed man who wants him gone. Ask: *"He has seconds. What does he offer before he asks for anything?"*

**What Jane noticed:**
- What this man cared about most at that moment: his golf game.
- The situation's limits: a retired boss in broad daylight on a golf course, with something to lose.

**What he did:**
1. Stayed calm and unhurried, which lowered the threat.
2. **Gave value first**: a genuinely useful swing tip, before asking any questions.
3. Only asked about the case after the man had relaxed.

## The technique

**Rapport = lower the threat + give value first + then ask.** Notice what Jane *didn't* do: flatter him, copy his posture or pretend to be his friend.

## Science check

- Giving first creates a pull to give back: **reciprocity** is one of the best-documented influence principles (Cialdini, Influence).
- Rapport research favours genuine attention over tricks: being echoed in your own words raised tips in a restaurant study (van Baaren et al. 2003), while deliberate body mirroring is a weaker, context-dependent effect (Förster et al. 2022).
- **Where TV cheats:** walking up to an armed crime boss is a terrible idea. Real de-escalation is done by trained negotiators, slowly.

## Try it

Before your next ask of someone (a favour, a question, help with something), give one genuinely useful thing first, with no strings: a tip, a resource, some real help. Then ask, and notice the difference.
