# Scene 06: Rigsby's memory palace, and the missed guesses about Lisbon

- **Episode:** *The Mentalist*, S2E11 "Rose-Colored Glasses" (CBS, first aired 14 Jan 2010)
- **Used in:** Lesson 7 (the memory palace); the missed guesses also work in Lesson 2 as an honest "misses happen" moment
- **Confidence:** the memory-palace coaching is confirmed directly on IMDb's plot summary and in an episode recap. The guesses about Lisbon are confirmed by the fan wiki and a recap.
- **Tell it like this:** in your own words, never as dialogue. Spoiler level: low.

## Moment A: the memory palace

A couple is murdered just before their 15-year high-school reunion. To flush out the killer, Jane wants Rigsby to go to the reunion posing as a long-gone former student who looks like him. That means Rigsby has only hours to learn the details of 67 reunion attendees. Jane teaches him to build a memory palace: each person's information is placed at a spot in a room Rigsby already knows well. During the reunion, Rigsby "walks" through that room in his mind to recall who's who.

**Stop point (for lessons):** stop when Rigsby is handed the 67 files and a few hours. Ask: *"How would YOU try to remember 67 people by tonight?"* Let them answer before revealing Jane's method.

**What Jane noticed:** Rigsby needed lots of unfamiliar facts fast, and he already had a perfect memory structure: a room he knew by heart.

**What he did:** coached the method of loci step by step: a familiar place, fixed spots, information attached to each spot, then recall by walking.

## Moment B: the missed guesses

At the reunion, Jane amuses himself by describing who Lisbon was as a teenager: ambitious, keeping both girls and boys at arm's length, unsure what she really wanted, and in the school band. She tells him he's wrong. At the closing dance he guesses a different instrument, and he's wrong again.

**What it teaches:** broad personality statements are hard to disprove, but specific claims (the exact instrument) fail visibly. Even Jane misses, and a good reader keeps guessing and updating instead of pretending.

## Science check

- **The memory palace really works.** Top memory competitors had ordinary brains and general ability; most of them used this method (Maguire et al. 2003). Ordinary adults who trained it 30 minutes a day for 40 days went from about 26 to about 62 remembered words (out of 72), and four months later still recalled about 48 (Dresler et al. 2017).
- **Honest limit:** it helps with the kind of material you train (names, lists, sequences), not memory in general. And 67 people in a few hours is TV speed; a beginner should start with 10 items.
- **The misses:** a reader's broad statements land because of the Barnum effect (Forer 1949); the specifics are where real accuracy shows.

## Try it

Build a 10-spot palace in your home and memorise a 10-item list. Recall it forwards, then backwards. (Lesson 7 walks you through it.)
