# Technique: Rapport and mirroring

_Character-free technique note. Source keys refer to `references/sources.md`._

## What it is

Rapport is the feeling of "this person gets me". People share more, cooperate more and trust more when they feel it. Reading people well depends on rapport, because guarded people give you nothing to read.

## How to build it

1. **Lower the stakes first.** Open with something light and genuine: a real observation, a small joke at your own expense. Not a pickup line.
2. **Match energy, not movements.** Roughly match their pace, volume and mood. A calm person doesn't want your hype; an excited person doesn't want your flatness.
3. **Mirror lightly and late.** Natural mimicry of posture and gestures happens between people who like each other (the "chameleon effect", Chartrand & Bargh 1999). If you do it on purpose, keep it subtle and delayed by a few seconds; obvious copying feels creepy.
4. **Use their words.** Repeating someone's own key phrases back ("so it felt *unfair*") signals listening. In a Dutch restaurant study, a waitress got bigger tips when she repeated customers' orders back word for word instead of paraphrasing (van Baaren et al. 2003). That's verbal mirroring, not copying posture.
5. **Ask about them, then listen fully.** Follow-up questions on *their* answer beat changing the topic to yourself.
6. **Give a real reason.** In the famous photocopier study, even an empty "because" helped for a tiny favour, but for a bigger ask only a *real* reason worked (Langer et al. 1978), and a later study failed to replicate the pattern (Folkes 1985). The lesson: give genuine reasons; "because" isn't a magic word.
7. **Name what you notice, kindly.** "You seem tired today" (if true and safe) shows attention.

## Science check

- **Works:** being mimicked made interactions feel smoother and partners more liked (Chartrand & Bargh 1999), and verbatim echoing earned bigger tips (van Baaren et al. 2003).
- **Smaller than claimed:** a 2022 replication found people copied smiles but not foot shaking or face rubbing (Förster et al. 2022). Deliberate body mirroring is a weak, context-dependent effect, and it backfires when noticed. Listening, matched energy and using their words matter more.
- **TV exaggeration:** instant total trust from a stranger in one sentence. Real rapport builds over minutes and depends on genuineness.
- **Myth:** NLP-style claims that matching someone's eye movements or "representational system" gives you control over them. That is not supported by research.

## Ethics line

Rapport is for connection and honest conversations. Faking closeness to extract secrets or money from a specific person is manipulation; the skill won't coach that.

## Drill ideas

- **Energy match:** the agent writes in different moods; the learner replies in matching energy, and the agent scores the fit.
- **Echo practice:** in a real conversation today, reflect two of the other person's own words back once each; notice what happens.
- **Follow-up ladder:** ask three follow-up questions in a row on someone's answer before saying anything about yourself.
