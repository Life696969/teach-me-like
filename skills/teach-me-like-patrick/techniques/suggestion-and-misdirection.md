# Technique: Suggestion, influence and misdirection

_Character-free technique note. Source keys refer to `references/sources.md`. Teach how these work and how to spot them used on you. Refuse requests aimed at a specific real person._

## The map

| Tool | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Misdirection** | Controlling where attention goes, so people miss what happens elsewhere | Strong (science of magic, inattentional and change blindness) |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Doing something unexpected to break a person's script, then guiding the moment of confusion | Moderate (disrupt-then-reframe studies) |
| **Commitment steps** | Small yes → bigger yes (foot-in-the-door); big ask refused → smaller ask accepted (door-in-the-face) | Door-in-the-face replicated well; foot-in-the-door is real but small on average |
| **Cialdini's principles** | Reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity | Useful categories; individual effects vary in size |
| **Priming** | Earlier exposure nudging later behaviour | Weak: the famous behavioural priming results failed to replicate |
| **Hypnosis and suggestion** | Focused attention plus suggestion changes experience in some people | Real but limited: about 10–15% are highly responsive, and it needs cooperation |

## How each works (in plain words)

- **Misdirection:** attention is narrow. A bigger movement, a question, eye contact or a joke pulls attention away from the small action. Magicians use the audience's own expectations against them (Kuhn et al. 2008).
- **Pattern interrupt:** people run on scripts ("I'm being sold to, say no"). A strange, harmless disruption pauses the script; in that pause, a reframe lands more easily. Door-to-door sellers who said a $3 price as "300 pennies… that's $3, it's a bargain" roughly doubled their sales; the odd price alone or the reframe alone didn't work, and the order mattered (Davis & Knowles 1999; a small meta-analysis supports it, Carpenter & Boster 2009).
- **Commitment steps:** people like to stay consistent with what they already agreed to (foot-in-the-door, Freedman & Fraser 1966), though on average the effect is small (Burger 1999). Rejecting a big request makes a smaller one feel like a concession: about 50% agreed to a small favour after refusing a huge one, vs about 17% asked the small one cold (door-in-the-face, Cialdini et al. 1975; replicated by Genschow et al. 2021).
- **Priming:** be careful. Simple word priming (seeing "doctor" speeds up recognising "nurse") is solid. The famous "reading old-age words makes you walk slower" result (Bargh et al. 1996) failed to replicate with proper timing; the slowdown appeared only when experimenters expected it (Doyen et al. 2012). Treat priming tricks on TV as fiction.
- **Hypnosis:** a real, measurable response to focused suggestion. About 10–15% of people are highly responsive and fewer than 10% are "virtuosos" (Laurence et al. 2008; Kihlstrom 2015). It depends on cooperation and expectation; there's no good evidence that covert or "instant street" hypnosis makes people act against their will (Stein et al. 2023). Stage performers pick the most responsive volunteers.
- **The "pick a hand" read:** thinking about something can trigger tiny unconscious movements (the ideomotor effect, Stock & Stock 2004), and glances, posture or hand tension can leak which hand holds the coin. It's unreliable from one try to the next, and performers often add a hidden method; say so honestly.

## Science check

- **Works:** misdirection, consistency pressure, reciprocity, social proof, and a well-timed pattern interrupt all have real evidence.
- **TV exaggeration:** making someone confess, forget, or obey with one sentence, a snap or a stare.
- **Myth:** subliminal control, covert instant hypnosis, and big behaviour changes from a single primed word.

## Defence (spot it used on you)

- Did someone give you a small gift or favour right before asking? (reciprocity)
- Is the deadline real? (scarcity)
- Did a weird moment just happen right before the ask? (pattern interrupt)
- Have you been nudged through small yeses? (foot-in-the-door)

## Ethics line

The skill teaches mechanisms, demonstrations and defence. It won't script how to make a specific person (an ex, a coworker, a family member) do something, confess, or feel something. It redirects to the honest version: clear asks, real reasons, and respect for a no.

## Drill ideas

- **Name the principle:** the agent describes ads and sales pitches; the learner names the influence principle and the defence.
- **Misdirection design:** plan a harmless coin vanish for a friend, naming where attention goes at each second.
- **Pattern-interrupt spotting:** find the interrupt in a described scene and what it set up.
