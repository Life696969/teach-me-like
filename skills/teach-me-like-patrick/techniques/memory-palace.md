# Technique: The memory palace (method of loci)

_Character-free technique note. Source keys refer to `references/sources.md`. This is the most reliable technique in the course: the science supports it strongly, so it makes a great early "it actually works" win._

## What it is

You remember things by placing vivid images of them along a route through a place you already know well (your home, your school, your street). To recall, you walk the route in your mind and "see" each item where you left it. It works because human memory is very good at places and images and weak at lists of abstract words.

## How to build your first palace

1. **Pick a place you know cold.** Your home works best for a first palace.
2. **Fix a route** and always walk it in the same direction: front door → shoe rack → sofa → TV → kitchen counter → fridge → … Choose 10 distinct spots (loci).
3. **Turn each item into a vivid image.** Make it big, absurd, moving, sensory, ideally funny or gross. "Milk" becomes a cow spraying milk all over your shoe rack.
4. **Make the image interact with the spot.** The item must *do something* to the location, not just sit there.
5. **Walk the route once to review,** right away, then again an hour later and the next day.
6. **Recall by walking.** Don't try to "remember the list"; walk the route and look.

## Levels

- **Level 1:** a 10-item shopping list.
- **Level 2:** names and faces. Link a feature of the face to an image of the name, and place it in a palace for that event.
- **Level 3:** a 20-item list, or a speech's key points in order.
- **Level 4:** a deck-of-cards order. Here memory athletes use a fixed image for each card plus long routes.

## Science check

- **Works, strongly:** the method of loci is an ancient technique with solid modern evidence. Top memory competitors didn't differ from ordinary people in general ability or brain structure; most of them used this method (Maguire et al. 2003). Ordinary adults who trained it 30 minutes a day for 40 days went from remembering about 26 to about 62 words out of 72, and four months later still recalled about 48 (Dresler et al. 2017). Classic lab work explains why vivid, interacting images at familiar places work (Bower 1970).
- **Honest limit:** the gain is for the *kind* of material you train (lists, names, sequences). It doesn't upgrade your memory in general.
- **TV exaggeration:** perfect, instant recall of everything ever seen. Palaces need deliberate encoding and review; you only remember what you placed.
- **Myth:** that it helps understanding. It helps you *remember* ordered items; understanding still needs studying.

## Common mistakes

- Images that are too plain ("a bottle of milk on the rack") fade.
- Reusing the same palace for many lists too quickly mixes them up; use several palaces or wait.
- Skipping the review walk.

## Drill ideas

- **10-item list:** the agent gives 10 items; the learner builds the images and places, the agent hides the list, and the learner recalls. Score it, then try backwards; backwards recall is the "wow" moment.
- **Name game:** 5 fictional people with names and one striking feature each; recall them after 10 minutes.
- **Teach-back:** the learner teaches the method to a friend using the friend's own home; teaching locks it in.
