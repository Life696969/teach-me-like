# Lesson 2: How "psychics" do it

- **The learner walks away able to:** build a cold reading, and spot one being done on them.
- **Scene:** `scenes/03-seeing-red-psychic.md` (warm-up option: `scenes/04-red-sauce-golf.md`, Moment A) · **Technique:** `techniques/cold-reading.md` · **About 20 minutes**

## 1. Hook: read them first

Say you're going to do a quick "reading" of them. Give these three lines, slowly and confidently, adapted to their language:
- "You can be very sociable, but there are times you really need to be alone to recharge."
- "You have a lot of potential you haven't fully used yet."
- "You're careful about who you trust, because you've been let down before."

Ask them to rate how accurate it felt, from 0 to 5. **Wait.** Then reveal: these lines fit almost everyone. In 1948 a psychology professor gave 39 students one identical "personal" profile, mostly copied from an astrology book. They rated it 4.26 out of 5 (Forer 1949). That's the Barnum effect, and it just worked on them.

## 2. The Jane moment

Tell scene 03 up to its stop point (the psychic's accurate reading of Jane). Ask: *"She got his past right. Is she psychic? What's the simplest explanation?"* **Wait.** Reveal: his family's murder was public news; she researched him. That's **hot reading**. Then tell the séance trap briefly, and end with the closing moment: even Jane, who knows every trick, is moved by her message about his daughter.

## 3. The technique: the cold reader's toolkit

Teach these as moves to **recognise** first, then to **perform openly as a demo**.
1. **Barnum statements:** they feel personal but fit anyone.
2. **Rainbow ruse:** name a trait and its opposite. "You're generous, but you've learned to protect yourself."
3. **High-probability guesses:** facts common for this person's age and situation. A student is probably stressed about exams or money; a 50-year-old has probably lost an older relative.
4. **Fishing:** a question dressed as a statement. "I'm getting a name with an S or an R…" and the person fills it in.
5. **Shotgunning:** many quick guesses; build on the hits, drop the misses. People remember the hits.
6. **Reading reactions:** nods, leaning in, pauses and wet eyes tell the reader where to go next.
7. **Hot reading:** information gathered beforehand (social media, gossip), presented as a gift.

**The ethical way to use it: demonstrate, then reveal.** The reveal is the most impressive part anyway.

## 4. Science check

- **Works:** the Barnum effect is robust and widely replicated (Forer 1949). The toolkit is documented by people who investigated psychics (Hyman 1977; Rowland 1998). Hyman once believed in palm reading, until he tried giving people the *opposite* readings and found they landed just as well.
- **TV:** cold reading producing specific hidden facts (an exact name, an address) reliably. Specifics usually come from fishing or prior research.
- **Myth:** that good readers have a special gift. It's a learnable script plus how human memory works: we remember the hits.
- **Honest misses:** even Jane misses. At a reunion he confidently guesses teenage Lisbon's instrument, twice, and is wrong both times (scene 06, Part B). Broad statements survive; specific ones fail visibly.

## 5. Drill

**Part 1: Barnum or not?** Give them all six and ask them to label each as Barnum, high-probability guess or a specific claim. **Wait.**

1. "You have a lot of unused potential." → **Barnum.**
2. "You were born in a city of over 5 million people." → **A specific claim.** Testable, and often false.
3. "Sometimes you're outgoing, but other times you just want to be alone." → **Barnum (rainbow ruse).**
4. "You have a scar on your knee from childhood." → **High-probability guess.** Lots of people do, and it feels uncanny when it lands.
5. "Someone close to you has been on your mind lately." → **Barnum / fishing.** Always true; it invites them to supply the name.
6. "You're left-handed." → **A specific claim.** Low probability (most people aren't), so a reader would avoid it.

**Part 2: write a reading.** Use the commuter from Lesson 1 (Case A). Write a 5-line "psychic reading" of him: two lines from real clues, two Barnum lines and one fishing line. Ask them to label each line. **Wait.** Feedback: did the clue-based lines use real clues? Is the fishing line open enough for him to fill in?

## 6. Field exercise

Watch one tarot, astrology or "psychic reading" video or reel. Tag every move you hear: Barnum, rainbow, fishing, shotgun, high-probability guess, hot reading. Count the misses the reader skips past.

**Optional, with a friend:** do a three-line reading, ask them to rate it 0 to 5, then **reveal how you did it**.

**Ethics line:** never use cold reading to claim real psychic powers, to take money, or on someone who is grieving. Demonstrate, then reveal.

## 7. Self-check

1. What's the difference between cold reading and hot reading? *Cold reading works live from clues and safe statements; hot reading uses information gathered in advance.*
2. Why do readings feel accurate even when they're generic? *The Barnum effect, plus we remember hits and forget misses.*
3. Someone "knows" something about you. What two questions do you ask yourself? *Did I just tell them? Could they have looked it up?*
