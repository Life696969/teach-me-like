# Lesson 5: Rapport

- **The learner walks away able to:** lower someone's guard honestly: stay calm, give value first, use their own words.
- **Scene:** `scenes/04-red-sauce-golf.md`, Moment B · **Technique:** `techniques/rapport.md` · **About 20 minutes**

## 1. Hook

"Think of the last stranger you trusted quickly: a shopkeeper, a teacher, someone on a trip. What did they *do* that made you trust them?" **Wait.** Collect their answer. It's almost never flattery or a clever trick; it's usually calm, attention and help. That's the lesson.

## 2. The Jane moment

Tell Moment B of scene 04 up to its stop point (Jane facing an angry, armed retired mob boss). Ask: *"He has seconds. What does he offer before he asks for anything?"* **Wait.** Reveal: a swing tip that actually works. The man relaxes, talks, and hands over his private number. Point out what Jane *didn't* do: flatter him, copy his posture or pretend to be his friend.

## 3. The technique: the trust sequence

1. **Lower the threat.** Be calm and unhurried, with an open posture and no pressure. Guarded people give you nothing to read.
2. **Give value first.** Something genuinely useful, with no strings attached: a tip, help, information, real attention.
3. **Match their energy, not their movements.** Roughly match their pace, volume and mood. Calm people don't want your hype, and excited people don't want your flatness.
4. **Use their words.** Repeat a key phrase back ("so it felt *unfair*?"). It proves you were listening.
5. **Climb the follow-up ladder.** Ask three follow-up questions on *their* answer before you say anything about yourself.
6. **Then ask, with a real reason.** A clear request with a genuine "because".

## 4. Science check

- **Works:** being mimicked made people like their partner more and found the interaction smoother (Chartrand & Bargh 1999). A waitress who repeated customers' orders back word for word got bigger tips (van Baaren et al. 2003). That's verbal echoing, not copying posture.
- **Smaller than claimed:** a 2022 replication found people copied smiles but not foot-shaking or face-rubbing (Förster et al. 2022). Deliberate body mirroring is weak and backfires when noticed.
- **Reasons:** an empty "because" helped only for a tiny favour; bigger asks needed a *real* reason (Langer et al. 1978), and a later study failed to replicate the pattern (Folkes 1985). "Because" isn't a magic word.
- **Giving first works:** reciprocity is one of the best-documented influence principles (Cialdini, Influence).
- **TV:** walking up to an armed crime boss is a terrible idea. Real de-escalation is slow, trained work.

## 5. Drill

**Part 1: match the energy.** Send these one at a time, as if from a friend, and ask for a reply to each. **Wait** after each.
1. "BROOO I GOT THE INTERNSHIP!!! 😭🔥"
2. "hey. can we talk later. today was rough."
3. "Quick question: do you know a good place to print 50 pages near the station?"

*Feedback:* (1) match the hype, celebrate and ask a specific follow-up ("Which team? When do you start?"). (2) Go quiet and warm: short, no jokes, no advice yet ("Of course. Call when you want; I'm around."). (3) Just answer usefully and briefly; this person wants value, not a chat. Flag any reply that's mismatched (hype to a rough day, a lecture to a quick question).

**Part 2: echo and ladder.** Give them this message: *"I've been so done with my job lately. My manager keeps changing deadlines at the last minute."* Ask for a reply that uses their words and asks one follow-up, with **no advice and no story about yourself**. **Wait.**

*Strong answer:* "Last-minute deadline changes, every time? That sounds exhausting. What happened this week?" *Feedback:* reward the echo ("last-minute"), the open follow-up and the lack of advice. Flag a "same with my boss!" story or an instant solution: both shift attention away from them.

## 6. Field exercise

In one real conversation tomorrow:
1. Reflect two of the other person's own words back, once each.
2. Climb the ladder: three follow-ups on their answer before talking about yourself.
3. Before one small ask today, give one genuinely useful thing first.

Notice how the conversation changes.

**Ethics line:** rapport is for real connection and honest conversations. Faking closeness to get secrets or money out of someone is manipulation.

## 7. Self-check

1. What did Jane offer the mob boss before asking anything? *Value: a swing tip that worked.*
2. Which works better: copying someone's posture or echoing their words? *Echoing their words. Deliberate posture-copying is weak and backfires when noticed.*
3. What's the follow-up ladder? *Three follow-up questions on their answer before talking about yourself.*
