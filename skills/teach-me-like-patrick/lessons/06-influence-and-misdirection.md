# Lesson 6: Influence and misdirection

- **The learner walks away able to:** name the influence move being used on them, and plan a harmless misdirection trick.
- **Scene:** `scenes/05-twenty-heads.md` · **Technique:** `techniques/suggestion-and-misdirection.md` · **About 25 minutes**

## 1. Hook

"I flip a coin twenty times. Heads every time. What are the odds?" **Wait.** Answer: 1 in 2^20, about one in a million (1 in 1,048,576). "So is it magic, or is something about the setup not what it seems?"

## 2. The Jane moment

Tell scene 05 up to its stop point (the twentieth heads). Ask: *"List every possible explanation, from most boring to most magical."* **Wait.** Then reveal: two quarters, one double-headed, swapped. Point out the real trick: by claiming powers, Jane pointed everyone at "how can he do that?" instead of "is that a normal coin?"

## 3. The technique

**Part A: misdirection.** Attention is narrow. A bigger movement, a question, eye contact, a joke or a *story* pulls attention away from the small action that matters. Magicians plan where your attention goes at every second.

**Part B: the influence map.** Teach each move together with its defence.

| Move | What it looks like | Defence question |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | A free sample, a small favour, then the ask | "Am I saying yes because I got something first?" |
| **Scarcity** | "Only 2 left!" or "Offer ends tonight" | "Is this deadline real?" |
| **Social proof** | "Join 2 million others" | "Do these people have the same needs as me?" |
| **Authority** | A white coat, a title, "experts recommend" | "Expert in *what*, exactly?" |
| **Liking / unity** | "People like us…", shared identity, flattery | "Would I agree if a stranger asked?" |
| **Foot-in-the-door** | A small yes, then a bigger ask | "Would I agree to the big ask on its own?" |
| **Door-in-the-face** | A huge ask, refused, then a "smaller" one | "Is the second ask actually small?" |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Something odd breaks your script, then a reframe | "What just happened right before the ask?" |

**Part C: what TV gets wrong.**
- **Hypnosis** is real but limited: about 10–15% of people are highly responsive, and it needs their cooperation. There's no good evidence for covert "instant" hypnosis making people act against their will.
- **Priming:** the famous "old-age words make you walk slower" result failed to replicate.

## 4. Science check

- **Strong:** misdirection is a studied toolkit for steering attention (Kuhn et al. 2008); about half of viewers missed a gorilla walking through a scene (Simons & Chabris 1999).
- **Replicated well:** door-in-the-face. About 50% agreed to a small favour after refusing a huge one, vs about 17% asked cold (Cialdini et al. 1975); a direct replication succeeded (Genschow et al. 2021).
- **Real but small:** foot-in-the-door (Freedman & Fraser 1966; Burger 1999).
- **Pattern interrupt:** selling note cards as "300 pennies… that's $3, a bargain" roughly doubled sales. The odd price alone or the reframe alone didn't work (Davis & Knowles 1999; Carpenter & Boster 2009).
- **Failed:** behavioural priming (Bargh et al. 1996 did not replicate in Doyen et al. 2012).
- **Hypnosis:** about 10–15% highly responsive (Laurence et al. 2008); no good evidence it overrides will (Stein et al. 2023).

## 5. Drill

**Part 1: name the move.** Give all six; for each, ask for the move and the defence question. **Wait.**
1. A hotel site: "Only 2 rooms left at this price! 14 people are looking right now." → *Scarcity + social proof.*
2. A shop gives you a free chai while you browse sarees. → *Reciprocity.*
3. A charity asks you to volunteer every weekend for a year; you refuse; they ask for one Sunday. → *Door-in-the-face.*
4. A toothpaste ad: a person in a white coat says "dentists recommend". → *Authority (check: which dentists, how many?).*
5. A friend asks you to sign a small online petition; next week they ask you to join a protest march. → *Foot-in-the-door (fine if you agree with it; just notice the step).*
6. A street seller says "Not 500, not 400… for you, only 299!" → *Pattern interrupt: a confusing countdown breaks your script, then the reframe lands ("for you, only…").*

**Part 2: plan a misdirection.** Ask them to plan a harmless coin vanish for a friend: the coin secretly stays in the right hand while the left hand pretends to take it. Have them write, second by second, **where the friend's attention should be** and what draws it there. **Wait.**

*Strong answer:* the attention follows the "taking" hand (eyes on it, a pause, a squeeze, a question to the friend). The right hand relaxes naturally and drops out of attention. The reveal opens the left hand. *Feedback:* reward a plan that uses the eyes, a question or a story to steer attention. Remind them: **always reveal how it worked afterwards.**

## 6. Field exercise

1. Spot three influence moves in ads, apps or shops today, and say the defence question to yourself.
2. Perform your coin vanish (or any simple trick) for a friend, then explain the misdirection.

**Ethics line:** learn these to spot them and to demonstrate them. Don't use them to make a specific person do something they wouldn't freely choose. If a request is aimed at a real person ("how do I make my coworker…"), the honest version is a clear ask, a real reason, and accepting a no.

## 7. Self-check

1. Why did Jane say he had powers? *To point attention at the wrong question: "how?" instead of "is the coin normal?"*
2. "Only 3 left!" What's the move, and the defence? *Scarcity; ask whether the deadline is real.*
3. Can someone be secretly hypnotised into doing something against their will? *There's no good evidence for it. Hypnosis needs cooperation, and only about 10–15% of people are highly responsive.*
