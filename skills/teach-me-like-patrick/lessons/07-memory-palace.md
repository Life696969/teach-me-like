# Lesson 7: The memory palace

- **The learner walks away able to:** remember a 10-item list forwards and backwards.
- **Scene:** `scenes/06-rigsby-memory-palace.md`, Moment A · **Technique:** `techniques/memory-palace.md` · **About 25 minutes**

## 1. Hook: baseline score

Give this list once, then ask them to look away and write down as many as they can, in order. **Wait.** Record their score, and note any items in the wrong order.

> mango · umbrella · phone charger · lion · cricket bat · toothpaste · guitar · rocket · ice cream · old shoe

"Hold that number. In twenty minutes we beat it, backwards too."

## 2. The Jane moment

Tell Moment A of scene 06 up to its stop point (Rigsby is handed 67 files and a few hours). Ask: *"How would YOU try to remember 67 people by tonight?"* **Wait.** Then reveal Jane's method: put each person's information at a spot in a room Rigsby already knows by heart, then walk through the room in his mind.

## 3. The technique: build your first palace

1. **Pick a place you know cold.** Your home is perfect for a first palace.
2. **Fix a route with 10 spots,** always walked in the same direction. Example: front door → shoe rack → sofa → TV → dining table → kitchen counter → fridge → bathroom mirror → bed → window.
3. **Turn each item into a vivid image:** huge, absurd, moving, noisy, funny or gross.
4. **Make the image *do something* to the spot.** Not "a mango on the shoe rack", but "a giant mango has burst all over the shoe rack and juice is dripping into your shoes".
5. **Walk the route once to review** straight away, then again in an hour and tomorrow.
6. **To recall, walk; don't "remember the list".** Look at each spot and see what's there.

## 4. Science check

- **Works, strongly:** top memory competitors had ordinary brains and general ability, and most of them used this method (Maguire et al. 2003). Ordinary adults who trained 30 minutes a day for 40 days went from about 26 to about 62 remembered words, and still recalled about 48 four months later (Dresler et al. 2017). Classic research explains why vivid, interacting images at familiar places work (Bower 1970).
- **Honest limit:** it helps for the kind of material you train (lists, names, sequences), not memory in general, and it helps you *remember*, not *understand*.
- **TV:** 67 people in a few hours is TV speed. Real memory athletes build up to that over months.

## 5. Drill: build it live

1. Ask them to name their 10 spots in their own home, in order. **Wait.** Check: are the spots distinct and in a walkable order?
2. Go item by item (mango → spot 1, and so on). For each, ask for their image. **Wait** each time. Upgrade weak images: make them bigger, moving, sensory, interacting with the spot. Example upgrades:
   - *phone charger at the TV:* the cable is a snake strangling the TV and sparking.
   - *lion on the dining table:* a lion is eating your dinner and roars when you sit down.
   - *rocket at the bathroom mirror:* a rocket launches out of the mirror and shatters it, with smoke everywhere.
3. When all 10 are placed, ask them to walk the route once in their head.
4. **Test:** move to something else for a minute (ask them about their day), then ask for the list **forwards**. **Wait.** Then **backwards**, walking the route in reverse. **Wait.**
5. Compare with the hook score. Celebrate specifically: backwards recall is the "wow" moment, because rote memory can't do it easily and a palace can.

If they miss an item, go to that spot and make the image stranger; misses almost always come from a plain image.

## 6. Field exercise

1. Tomorrow, recall today's list again by walking the palace, without looking.
2. Use a *second* palace (your college, office or street) for a real list: shopping, a speech's key points or a day's tasks.
3. **Names:** next time you meet someone, link a striking feature of theirs to an image of their name, and place it at a spot in a "people" palace.

**Tip:** don't reuse the same palace for a new list too quickly, or the lists blur together. Build more palaces.

## 7. Self-check

1. Why does a vivid, absurd image work better than a plain one? *Memory grabs onto the unusual, the sensory and the emotional; plain images fade.*
2. How do you recall a list backwards? *Walk the route in reverse.*
3. Does a memory palace improve your memory in general? *No. It works for the kind of material you use it for, and it helps remembering, not understanding.*
